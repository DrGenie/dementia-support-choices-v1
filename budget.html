Budget planner

For a given public budget, find the programme design and fee that give the people offered the most value, and compare it with the programme most people would take up.

Budget and costs

Share of the people offered who can be given a place. If more people want a place, places are allocated at random.
Programme types the planner may choose

Weekly programmes only. Virtual reality programmes have no CHSP price and are not included.

Highest stated value within the budget

The line shows the highest value to users that each budget can buy. Value is the amount people offered would give up, in fees, to keep the programme, counted up to A$50 per session, the highest fee in the study.

How the planner works

Costs, assumptions and what the planner leaves out.

For every weekly programme design allowed in the study and every fee from A$0 to A$50, the planner predicts the share of people offered who would take the programme (the mixed logit with a piecewise fee effect) and the value to them. Public spending is the number of participants times the provider cost less the fee. The planner picks the design and fee with the most value within the budget. Fees are payments from users to the provider: they reduce public spending and are already subtracted from users' value, so they are not counted twice.

Provider costs come from the national unit price ranges of the Commonwealth Home Support Programme for 2025 to 2026: group social support for peer support and community engagement (per client-hour), specialised support services for counselling (per hour) and transport for programmes 50 km or more away (one return trip per in-person session, half that for hybrid). Two parts are assumptions: including the carer adds 25%, 50% or 100% of the base cost, and online delivery costs 60%, 75% or 100% of in-person delivery (low, central and high).

This is an illustration from a stated preference survey, not a cost-effectiveness analysis. It does not count health effects, benefits to carers beyond the person deciding, waiting lists or the cost of raising public funds.

About this website

Dementia Support Choices presents the results of a discrete choice experiment with 264 Australian adults, carried out by researchers at the University of Newcastle. It gives information from research. It is not medical advice and does not replace advice from health professionals, aged care assessors or service providers.
